## Projects section

**Name**: Hic — The Bayeux Tapestry, scene by scene

**Description (EN)**

Hic is a reading of the Bayeux Tapestry, scene by scene, in English and French, which I made while the embroidery
is on loan to the British Museum (September 2026 – July 2027). It does not offer new research. It brings together
what published scholarship says about each of the 58 scenes, and tries to keep apart three things that popular
accounts often blur: what the embroidery actually shows, what medieval writers report, and what historians infer or
still dispute. Every note cites its sources; where a question is open (Harold's oath, Edward's last words, the
"arrow in the eye"), it is presented as a debate rather than settled.

The image is the 2017 high-resolution photography, published in the public domain on Wikimedia Commons. The
narration is read by a synthetic voice (ElevenLabs), and the code was written with the help of an AI coding
assistant. The texts are my own syntheses and are still being checked against the works they cite. The project is
independent and non-commercial, with no advertising or tracking.

## Post (EN)

The Bayeux Tapestry is at the British Museum until 11 July 2027. I made a small web app to read it alongside the
visit, or from far away: Hic, the Bayeux Tapestry scene by scene.

It is not original research. It is an attempt to read the embroidery carefully, scene by scene, and to be clear
about the status of each statement: what the stitches show, what the medieval chroniclers say, and what historians
argue, often without agreeing.

What it contains:
· the 58 scenes on the 2017 photography, in deep zoom
· a narrated tour in English and French (synthetic voice), and a shorter tour of 15 moments
· the Latin captions, word by word
· some sixty notes with their sources, from the comet of 1066 to the debate over the "arrow in the eye"

The texts are my syntheses of the scholarship cited in the app, and I am still checking them. Corrections are
welcome.

The name comes from the captions: more than half of them begin with "hic", here.
