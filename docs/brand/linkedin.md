## Projects section

**Name**: Hic — The Bayeux Tapestry, scene by scene

**Description (EN)**

A bilingual web app for reading the Bayeux Tapestry from left to right, made for its loan to the British Museum
(September 2026 – July 2027). The whole embroidery in deep zoom; a narrated tour in English and French that
scrolls the strip for you; the Latin captions word by word; and some sixty sourced notes, from Halley's comet to
the "arrow in the eye", that keep apart what the embroidery shows, what medieval writers say and what historians
argue. Independent and non-commercial, with no ads, cookies or tracking.

Built with TypeScript, Vite, OpenSeadragon and the Web Audio API; content in YAML; narration voiced with
ElevenLabs; image tiles, media and audio produced by GitHub Actions; hosted on Cloudflare.

## Post (EN)

The Bayeux Tapestry has crossed the Channel: it is on show at the British Museum until 11 July 2027.

To go with it, I made Hic, a free web app for reading the embroidery the way it was meant to be read, from left
to right, scene by scene.

· All 58 scenes in deep zoom, down to single stitches
· A narrated tour in English and French that scrolls for you, or a 15-stop highlights tour
· The Latin captions, word by word
· Sixty-odd notes on history, technique, daily life and the Tapestry's afterlife, each with its sources, and
  debates shown as debates

It is independent and non-commercial: no ads, no cookies, no tracking.

The name comes from the captions themselves, more than half of which begin with "hic", here.
